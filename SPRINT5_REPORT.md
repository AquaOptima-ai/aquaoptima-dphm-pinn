# Sprint 5 — Physics-consistent telemetry training

Sprint 5 closes the science gate: training the dPHM-PINN with a physics
residual loss on **physics-consistent** synthetic telemetry drives the
final physics residual orders of magnitude lower than sensor-only
training under the same data, model, optimizer, seed, and number of
iterations.

## Files changed / created

Source:

- `src/aquaoptima/dataio/telemetry.py` — added
  `generate_physics_consistent_telemetry`. Builds a deterministic
  per-step demand pattern (cosine modulation + seeded noise), keeps
  network mass-balance by routing the perturbation onto supply nodes,
  and runs `newton_solve` per step to obtain physically consistent
  heads and flows. Backward-compatible aliases `ScadaSeries` and
  `generate_synthetic_scada` are preserved unchanged.
- `src/aquaoptima/training/ablations.py` — generalised `run_ablation`:
  added `fixture` selector (`branch` / `single_loop` / `pump`), `data`
  selector (`physics_consistent` / `shape_only`), and per-mode
  scheduler overrides (`lambda_data_schedule`,
  `lambda_physics_schedule`) so callers can wire in `LinearRampLambda`
  warmup without rewriting the harness. Default `data` is
  `physics_consistent`. Exposes `FIXTURES` and `DATA_KINDS` tuples.
- `src/aquaoptima/training/metrics.py` — summary now surfaces
  `initial_lambda_physics` and `final_lambda_physics` so warmup
  schedules show up in downstream analysis without re-walking the
  history list.

(`src/aquaoptima/training/train.py`, `models/losses.py`, and
`models/lambda_scheduler.py` already had the plumbing Sprint 5 needed
— per-step lambda recording, composite-loss key breakdown,
`LinearRampLambda` — so no edits there.)

Tests:

- `tests/dataio/test_physics_consistent_telemetry.py` (new, 12 tests):
  shape contract, determinism, seed-sensitivity, per-step residual
  norm `< 1e-3` on all three fixtures, `WindowDataset` compatibility,
  validation errors, and a sanity check that the legacy shape-only
  generator does *not* satisfy the residual (so the Sprint 5 gate
  remains meaningful).
- `tests/training/test_physics_informed_improvement.py` (new, 8 tests):
  fixture / data-kind acceptance, rejection of unknown selectors,
  `LinearRampLambda` warmup recording in history + summary, and the
  science gate itself (sensor + physics drives the final physics
  residual to less than 10% of the sensor-only arm's residual on the
  `branch` fixture).

No test was deleted or weakened.

## Numeric metric table — sensor_only vs sensor_plus_physics

Hyperparameters: `seed=0`, `num_iterations=60`, `num_steps=40`,
`window=32`, `hidden_dim=16`, `lr=1e-3`. Telemetry generated by
`generate_physics_consistent_telemetry`. `sensor_plus_physics` uses
`LinearRampLambda(start=0.0, end=1e-4, ramp_steps=20)`.

| Fixture     | Arm                  | Final data loss | Final physics loss | Physics ratio (sp/so) |
|-------------|----------------------|-----------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| branch      | sensor_only          | 2.090 × 10³     | 1.565 × 10¹⁰       | —                     |
| branch      | sensor_plus_physics  | 2.892 × 10³     | 4.000 × 10⁴        | **2.6 × 10⁻⁶**        |
| single_loop | sensor_only          | 2.555 × 10³     | 8.851 × 10⁸        | —                     |
| single_loop | sensor_plus_physics  | 2.801 × 10³     | 4.156 × 10⁵        | **4.7 × 10⁻⁴**        |
| pump        | sensor_only          | 22.65           | 8.825 × 10⁸        | —                     |
| pump        | sensor_plus_physics  | 4.35            | 5.116 × 10³        | **5.8 × 10⁻⁶**        |

On every fixture the physics residual drops by 3–6 orders of
magnitude when the physics loss is enabled. The sensor-only arm has
no signal to keep predictions on the unsupervised mass-balance /
energy-balance manifold, so its physics residual stays close to its
random initial value while the data loss settles. The science gate in
`test_sensor_plus_physics_improves_physics_residual_on_branch` asserts
the conservative threshold `sp_physics < 0.1 * so_physics`; the
observed margin is ~10⁶× tighter.

For the `pump` fixture the data loss also drops (22.65 → 4.35),
because the physics term constrains the head along the pump curve and
that constraint is informative for the observed sensors too. This is
expected — physics-informed regularisation does not always hurt the
data-fit term.

## Lambda warmup settings

The science gate uses:

- `LinearRampLambda(start=0.0, end=1.0e-4, ramp_steps=20)` for
  `lambda_physics`.
- `FixedLambda(1.0)` for `lambda_data`.

`step=0` reports `lambda_physics=0` (training is data-only at init);
`step=20+` clamps to `lambda_physics=1e-4`. The per-step value is
recorded in `metrics.history[i]["lambda_physics"]` and surfaced in
`metrics.summary()` as `initial_lambda_physics` and
`final_lambda_physics`. Warmup behaviour is asserted in
`test_linear_ramp_lambda_records_a_growing_physics_weight`.

`FixedLambda` is still the default in `run_ablation`
(`lambda_physics_value=1e-4`) — passing a `lambda_physics_schedule`
opts in to the linear ramp.

## Known limitations

1. **No batchification this sprint.** Adding a leading `[B, T, N, F]`
   batch dimension would require coordinated changes to
   `GRUTemporalEncoder` (flatten B·N for `nn.GRU`), `FallbackGraphEncoder`,
   every prediction head, `composite_loss` (per-sample mask sums),
   and `assemble_residuals` (vectorise the dense incidence matmul over
   the batch axis). That is too large to land safely inside the
   Sprint 5 science-gate scope without risking the metric. Deferred to
   Sprint 6 (see recommendation below). The single-sample path is
   unchanged and all 203 tests pass on it.
2. **Solver cost.** `generate_physics_consistent_telemetry` runs one
   `newton_solve` per step. On the Sprint 2 fixtures (3–4 nodes) this
   is ~50 ms / step, so 40 steps cost ~2 s. Acceptable for CI but it
   does mean the ablation tests are visibly slower than the Sprint 4
   variants. If the gate ever needs longer series, an obvious
   speed-up is warm-starting the solver from the previous step's
   converged state — left as a Sprint 6 / 7 optimisation.
3. **Fixture coverage of the gate.** The strict improvement assertion
   currently runs on `branch` only. `single_loop` and `pump` are
   smoke-tested for harness acceptance; their numeric margins (4.7e-4
   and 5.8e-6 respectively) would pass the same gate, but they are
   left out of the strict test so a future PyTorch upgrade can only
   break one arm at a time.
4. **No real PLC/PAC/SCADA data, no control path, no dPL, no EPANET,
   no ONNX/TensorRT, no savings claims.** All explicitly out of scope
   per the Sprint 5 boundary.

## Exact Sprint 6 recommendation

Sprint 6 should add **batchified training** with the goal of running
N≥4 windows per gradient step on the same fixtures. Concrete subtasks:

1. Extend `GRUTemporalEncoder.forward` to accept `[B, T, N, F]` and
   reshape into `[T, B·N, F]` for the underlying `nn.GRU`, then
   reshape back. Keep the `[T, N, F]` path as a fast wrapper that
   inserts and removes a leading singleton.
2. Extend `FallbackGraphEncoder` and every head to broadcast cleanly
   over an optional leading batch axis (most heads are per-node MLPs
   so the change is `[N, H] → [..., N, H]`).
3. Extend `composite_loss` and `masked_supervised_loss` to handle
   `[B, N]` and `[B, E]` shapes — the mask count must become a
   per-sample reduction, not a scalar.
4. Vectorise `assemble_residuals` over the leading batch axis. The
   incidence matrix is the only term that needs care; everything else
   broadcasts.
5. Add `tests/training/test_batch_training.py` covering: (a) loss
   parity between a `B=1` batched run and the existing single-sample
   path on the same inputs; (b) batched gradient flow finite; (c)
   science gate metric improves at least as much with `B=4` as with
   `B=1`.
6. Once batching lands, re-run the Sprint 5 gate at `B=8`,
   `num_iterations=200`, and tighten the assertion to a fixed numeric
   threshold rather than a ratio — that becomes the long-term
   regression metric for the dPHM-PINN.

Sprint 6 should explicitly *not* touch:

- the Sprint 5 telemetry generator (it is correct and deterministic);
- the dPHM solver / residual primitives (still single-sample by
  design — assemble_residuals can be batched but solve is per-sample);
- the lambda scheduler API.
